**$\sim$180 problems. 6 a day. 30 days.** Every problem sourced from or calibrated to real firm interviews at Citadel, Jane Street, Two Sigma, and others.

Two selection criteria: *(1)* problems that appear most frequently in real recent interviews, and *(2)* a structured progression so that each problem builds directly on the last. No wasted time on material firms do not test. No sudden difficulty cliff mid-preparation.

The course is not comprehensive. **It is precise.** Problems that did not make the cut were cut because top firms do not ask them. Mastery of the techniques within these 180 problems equips you to handle any interview problem confidently, including those you have never seen before.

# How to Study

## Attempt first, always

Spend real time on each problem before reading the solution. The struggle is not a detour from learning --- it is the learning. A solution read after genuine struggle restructures how you think about the entire topic. Use **active learning mode** (top-right profile menu) to lock solutions until you commit.

## Use the full problem-solving UI

Every problem links to Epigram's interactive problem-solving interface. Click the **$\nearrow$ button** to the right of the difficulty label on any problem card to open it. The UI includes multiple solution approaches, step-by-step hints, and deeper discussion beyond what appears in these pages. Use it whenever a solution leaves you wanting more.

## Study the solution, not just the answer

Ask four questions after every problem:

1.  What was the key insight?

2.  Where exactly did my approach diverge?

3.  What is the general pattern --- which recurring technique does this belong to?

4.  Can I explain this out loud in 60 seconds?

If you cannot do the fourth, you do not yet own the solution.

## Redo problems from scratch

Return to problems 3--7 days later, without looking at the solution. Prioritise: problems you got wrong, problems where your approach was slow, and problems that felt easy (these often conceal what interviewers probe).

## Get fluent with the techniques

Before moving to the next topic, be able to: state the technique in one sentence without notes, identify which technique applies to a new problem in under 30 seconds, and execute it on an unseen example. Knowing the theory without its application is the most common gap interviewers expose.
